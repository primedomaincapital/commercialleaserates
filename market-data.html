Commercial Lease Rates by Market and Property Type

The office market's best buildings have never charged more. Its average building has rarely charged less, relative to them. That split is the whole story.

Office: the recovery splits in two

JLL's Q2 2025 national office report reads like two markets sharing one vacancy statistic. Leasing volume held essentially flat, down 2.2% in the quarter, while the active pipeline of tenant requirements grew 5.8% quarter over quarter to its highest level since Q4 2021. Demand is back. It is just choosy: trophy buildings in Miami, New York, and San Francisco set record rents while older stock waits, and the average asking rent inside the construction pipeline — $92.38 per square foot — stands at the highest level on record by a substantial margin.

Industrial: the decline streak ends

Industrial tells a changed story. CBRE's Q3 2026 industrial figures show the national average net asking rent rising 3.6% year over year to $11.16 per sq. ft., with vacancy at 6.4% and net absorption of 104.6 million sq. ft. in the quarter — ending the run of quarterly declines the staged page described. The contrast with office narrows: industrial rents are rising again as leasing outpaces new supply.
